# Meaning

Within [documentation standards](../README.md), meaning determines what documentation says and what readers may rely on. Claims make individual propositions inspectable. Models organize those propositions around questions, concepts, and distinctions. Equations build exact answers from a small mathematical basis when that precision helps. Together, the three forms create a system readers can understand, apply, and check.

Meaning works with the other documentation pillars. [Structure](../structure/README.md) gives meaning a home, order, and links that delegate authority. [Style](../style/README.md) makes meaning clear without changing its force. [Quality](../quality/README.md) evaluates whether the documented meaning is correct and works for its readers. Meaning governs the semantic content and relationships that the other pillars arrange, express, and evaluate.

Meaning applies the aims defined in the [Pentagram manifesto](../../manifesto.md). Ergonomic meaning helps readers form a model they can reuse and helps authors see what a change affects. Deterministic meaning makes conclusions follow from explicit conditions and gives implementation and tests a shared semantic authority to realize and validate. Efficient meaning lets stable concepts do repeated explanatory work and states resource behaviour clearly enough to preserve efficiencies and repair waste.

## Claims

[Claims](claims.md) defines how documentation makes each proposition inspectable. It keeps the proposition's kind, status, scope, authority, conditions, and evidence relationship clear.

## Models

[Models](models.md) defines how documentation connects claims into a coherent account that answers declared questions. It identifies the concepts, contractual distinctions, dependencies, and allowed variation readers need to predict unfamiliar cases.

## Equations

[Equations](equations.md) defines a small mathematical notation for exact statements without imitating Pentagram code or prescribing an implementation. It distinguishes root equations from derived equations and shows how an equation can answer a model question directly or support an inspectable derivation. The same equations can govern different implementations.
