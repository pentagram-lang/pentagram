# Quality

Within [documentation standards](../README.md), quality asks whether documentation works. [Meaning](../meaning/README.md) establishes what readers may rely on. [Structure](../structure/README.md) gives that meaning a home. [Style](../style/README.md) makes it readable. Quality judges the result and records how strongly the evidence supports it.

Accuracy alone is not enough. A document fails when its readers cannot readily follow it, find what matters, or use it correctly. Fluent prose also fails when it hides false meaning. Quality must catch both failures.

The [Pentagram manifesto](../../manifesto.md) sets the bar. Good documentation reduces the effort of reading and change, makes meaning dependable, and exposes the costs that matter.

Quality begins with the applicable requirements and ends with a judgement over the complete evidence. Criteria governs that judgement. Lint, test, and review provide evidence with different strengths and limits. Gather the evidence in any useful order, then judge the whole result.

## Criteria

[Criteria](criteria.md) explains how to find the governing meaning, structure, and style requirements and apply them to the available evidence.

## Lint

[Lint](lint.md) defines reproducible checks for exact documentation rules. It also defines the boundary beyond which mechanical checking becomes unreliable.

## Test

[Test](test.md) puts documentation into realistic reader situations. Test companions record named tasks with their conditions and observable assertions for independent trials.

## Review

[Review](review.md) uses independent judgement to find defects that mechanical checks and reader trials cannot establish alone. Poor readability and needless reading work are always assessed separately; other lenses follow the review's consequence and uncertainty.
